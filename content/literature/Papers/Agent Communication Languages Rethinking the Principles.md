> [!garden] Summary
> In this article, Singh critiques [[Agent Communication Languages]] of the time like [[Arcol ACL]] and [[Knowledge Query and Manipulation Language|KQML]] for the mental agency approach and provides a better alternative focused on social agency. This approach focuses on combining "social commitments with a public perspective" through things like [[Norms in Agent Systems|Norms]] and a framework that focuses on when [[Communicative Acts]] are satisfied.

#### Notes

- To build effective [[Multi-Agent Systems]] where agents function together to either compete or cooperate, two key features are needed: [[Autonomous Agents|autonomy]] and interoperability
- Singh argues that current ACLs lack interoperability because they focus too much on the private perspective and [[Belief-Desire-Intention|mental agency]] of the individual agent
- He argues a [[Normative Concepts|normative]] approach to communication through things like social norms allows for more flexible systems were idiolects (language specific to one agent) won't form and agents with different dialects still operate effectively
  - _"The problem with traditional approaches is not the use of dialects per se, but that the dialects are arbitrary and cannot be adequately formalized in the chosen framework."_
- _"Our framework presupposes a richer infrastructure for agent management, which we term society management. This infrastructure supports the definition of commitments, roles, and groups, as well as operations for agents to join a society in specific roles, to change roles, and to exit the society."_
  - This sounds like the system proposed in [[Norms as a Basis or Governing Sociotechnical Systems Extended Abstract]]
- Singh breaks down 7 [[Communicative Acts]] based on Speech Act theory that encompass all communication. He argues that current approaches (Arcol and KQML) only include 2/7 of these (assertives and directives) and an ideal model would:
  - _"...assume that the ideal ACL would take a public perspective, emphasize conventional meaning, avoid pragmatics, consider context, and **include all major communicative acts**, you would be advocating a model that endorses social agency."_
