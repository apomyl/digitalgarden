> [!definition]
> An open network where [[Autonomous Agents]] and human participants act as peers, coordinating through shared social [[Norms in Agent Systems|norms]], technical protocols and joint [[Accountability in Agent Systems|accountability]].
>
> Formally in [[Norms as a Basis or Governing Sociotechnical Systems Extended Abstract]]:
>
> An organisation that includes two or more roles, each specified in terms of the [[Norms in Agent Systems|Norms]] applying to it. An organisation is defined via norms and a norm is defined in an organisation.

> [!definition] Open Network
> A party may live and act outside the scope of a sociotechnical system while remaining subject to the norms defined in the system

###### Maintaining an adaptive long-lived system

Two key principles are followed for adaptation:

- Centrality of Norms - a normative (social norms) rather than operational (step-by-step procedures) approach allows for minimal constraints while capturing the invariants of the system, allowing for a flexible yet rigorous solution.
- Autonomy and Policies - every participant is autonomous but subject to social norms, participants apply their own internal policies to decide how to interact given the norms.

> [!summary] Model from [[Norms as a Basis or Governing Sociotechnical Systems Extended Abstract|paper]]
> ![[Images/Sociotechnical Systems-1792264588407.webp|403]]
>
> Elements of a role façade
>
> - **Qualification** - a prerequisite or eligibility requirement for a principal to play the specified role
>   - e.g. a teacher has a teaching degree
> - **Privilege** - a liberty, broadly understood, accorded to a principal who plays the specified role
>   - e.g. a teacher has access to student data
> - **Liability** - a demand imposed on a principal who plays the  specified role
>   - e.g. a teacher is required to help students upon request
