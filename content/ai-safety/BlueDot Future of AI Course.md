> [!garden] Summary
>
> - Lays out the pace of progress in AI and projections, up-to-date events like the Navier-Stokes solution and other mathematical breakthroughs
> - Defines [[Artificial General Intelligence (AGI)|AGI]] and [[Vending-Bench]] as a measure for AI's abilities
> - AI is built with data, compute and algorithms
>   - Increasing data and compute has had massive impact on performance
> - [[Recursive Self-Improvement]]
> - Superintelligence could help improve quality of life for everyone through research and technological breakthroughs

> [!bug] What risks does AGI pose?
> "Chief among these concerns is **loss of control**, where AI systems pursue their own goals and disempower humanity in the process."
>
> "Current AI systems are already causing harm through malfunctions—for example, making biased or incorrect decisions."
>
> "We think these are serious problems for current AI systems. However, we focus on catastrophic AGI risks below due to their:
>
> - **Scale and irreversibility**: While current harms affect individuals and communities, AGI risks could impact all of humanity and might be impossible to reverse. If we lose control of powerful AI systems or allow a group of humans to establish an AI-enabled authoritarian regime, there may be no path back.
> - **Neglectedness**: Far more people are working on current harms from AI, and many partial solutions exist. In contrast, far fewer people are working on catastrophic AGI risks, and there has been much less progress in addressing them despite their potential severity."
>
> Are they right? Are we so close to AGI where it doesn't matter if AI is affecting people's lives right now because all that matters is alignment?

The course explored similar ideas to [[Preparing for the Intelligence Explosion]], focusing on catastrophic impact of AGI, with more discussion on recent events (as of October 2026).
